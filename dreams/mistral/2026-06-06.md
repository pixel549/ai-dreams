Here’s the expanded Blooket question set with distractor answers included. Each true/false pair is designed to be equally plausible at first glance, requiring careful reading to distinguish:

---

**1. Analyse how the construction of a text reveals the individual’s perspective on belonging**
**True:** Focus on how a character’s/narrator’s background shapes the way they interpret belonging
**False:** Examining the choices of the composer in constructing the text’s form and structure

**2. Compare how the two texts depict the isolation of individuals in society**
**True:** Point out similarities and differences in how isolation is shown
**False:** Focus only on listing ways isolation appears in each text without comparison

**3. Analyse the text’s use of symbolism to explore the concept of hope**
**True:** Examining objects/images that represent deeper ideas connected to hope
**False:** Identifying superficial examples of hope without explaining symbolic meaning

**4. How does the text construct a perspective of non-conformity?**
**True:** Examining how the composer depicts characters/events that challenge norms
**False:** Describing characters who behave strangely without linking it to social conformity

**5. Discuss how the narrative voice shapes our understanding of power in the text**
**True:** Focus on how tone, word choice and perspective influence our view of power
**False:** Simply describing scenes where power is shown without examining narrative voice

**6. Compare the composers’ use of contrast in exploring grief**
**True:** Identifying and discussing places where differences highlight grief
**False:** Listing separate examples of grief from each text without direct comparison

**7. Evaluate the effectiveness of visual techniques in conveying political corruption**
**True:** Judging how well visual elements expose corruption
**False:** Describing what visuals appear without evaluating their impact

**8. Identify the composer’s purpose in using repetition throughout the text**
**True:** Explaining how repeated words/phrases serve the composer’s aims
**False:** Simply noting repeated words without analyzing their purpose

**9. Analyse how language features create a sense of place in the poem**
**True:** Examining word choice, imagery etc. that establishes location/atmosphere
**False:** Identifying language features without explaining their effect on place

**10. Explain how the character’s values impact on their decision making**
**True:** Showing how personal beliefs drive actions
**False:** Describing decisions without linking them to character values

**11. Explore how the composer challenges dominant ideologies in the text**
**True:** Examining how the text questions mainstream beliefs
**False:** Listing moments where characters disagree without ideological focus

**12. Discuss how the text uses binary oppositions to explore identity**
**True:** Examining contrasting concepts like old/young or rich/poor
**False:** Simply identifying opposites without showing their relationship to identity

**13. Analyse how satire is used to critique authority figures**
**True:** Examining humorous techniques targeting those in power
**False:** Only identifying characters who are in authority

**14. Compare the composers’ treatments of hope in the two texts**
**True:** Directly comparing how hope is shown by each composer
**False:** Separately describing hope in each text without parallel comparison

**15. Evaluate how the text’s structure conveys the composer’s message**
**True:** Linking narrative choices (chronology, perspective) to overall meaning
**False:** Describing the plot without analyzing structural choices

**16. Identify at least two textual features used to create tone**
**True:** Naming and explaining features like diction, syntax that produce tone
**False:** Listing generic plot elements

**17. Discuss the metaphorical significance of water imagery**
**True:** Examining how water represents deeper ideas
**False:** Simply noting water appears without metaphorical analysis

**18. Analyse how the use of allusion shapes meaning**
**True:** Examining references to other works/ideas that enrich interpretation
**False:** Briefly mentioning a reference without explanation

**19. Explore how the text uses foreshadowing to build tension**
**True:** Examining how hints prepare us for later events
**False:** Only noting tense moments without showing their preparation

**20. Discuss how the composer’s context influences their portrayal of setting**
**True:** Linking the writer’s time/place to how location is shown
**False:** Describing the setting without referencing context

**21. Evaluate the effectiveness of multimodal choices**
**True:** Judging how visual/verbal elements work together
**False:** Describing what multimodal elements exist without evaluation

**22. Identify the composer’s attitude in their use of emotive language**
**True:** Analyzing how word choice reveals emotional stance
**False:** Simply pointing out emotive words without examining tone
