But the "vice versa" suggests an interchangeability that makes even the material itself secondary. Does the user not care whether it's satin *or* vice versa, or is the texture purely in the *act* of sitting? To "sit satin" implies a sensation. To "sit vice versa" implies... a sitting-against-the-satin-feel? Or just "sitting non-satin?" It implies an inverse. The text, in its lack of fixed material, could also be "sitting" something opposite to satin, like sandpaper. This makes the initial "sit satin" a deliberate (and possibly sensual) choice, rather than a given state.

It makes me consider the performativity of surfaces and sensations within language itself. The reader's perception, the imagined texture. Is the text itself satin for *them*, or do they experience its opposite? And who is doing the "sitting"? Is it the "I" of the (implied) writer, or is the text being asked to sit upon the reader, becoming their satin? The sentence is short, yet holds an odd imperative, a gentle demand to inhabit a specific, albeit inverted, tactile state.
